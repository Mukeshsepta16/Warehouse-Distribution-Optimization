Why distribution cost matters

India spends heavily on moving goods. The 2025 DPIIT–NCAER assessment puts national logistics cost for 2023-24 at ₹24.01 lakh crore, and transport is the largest part of it.

A distributor serves several stores from several warehouses. Routes differ in distance, warehouses hold limited stock and every store needs a fixed quantity. Serving each store from its “usual” warehouse ignores these cost differences. Official data has no route-wise cost, so each firm must analyse its own routes. That is a job for Operations Research.

Source: NCAER and DPIIT, Assessment of Logistics Cost in India, September 2025. The report has no FMCG-specific figures, so the FMCG setting is our case-study choice.

The mathematical model

Minimise Z = Σi Σj Cij Xij
Supply: Σj Xij ≤ Si
Demand: Σi Xij = Dj
Non-negativity: Xij ≥ 0

Xij is the units sent from warehouse i to store j, Cij the cost per unit on that route, Si the warehouse stock and Dj the store demand.

If supply and demand differ, a dummy warehouse (unmet demand) or dummy store (unused stock) balances the problem at zero cost.
